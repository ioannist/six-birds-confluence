\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A rewriting system is \emph{confluent} when the choices made during reduction do not matter in the end: whenever a term can be rewritten in two ways, the two results can be rewritten further to a common term \cite{BaaderNipkow1998,Huet1980,DershowitzJouannaud1990,Terese2003}. Discrete geometry has a principle with the same flavor. A connection is \emph{flat} when transport around every small, contractible loop returns what it started with, so that transport between two points is unchanged when the route is deformed with its endpoints fixed; the failure of flatness is measured by holonomy around such small loops \cite{Meneses2021,BaezMuniain1994}. Flatness is thus a local form of route independence; around loops that cannot be contracted, a flat connection may still have nontrivial holonomy. The resemblance between ``order does not matter'' and ``route does not matter'' is easy to state and easy to overstate. This paper asks what the resemblance amounts to when it is made exact.

The answer depends on choosing the right object. A first guess is to look for flatness in the reduction graph itself, by asking whether the graph has cycles or how many independent undirected cycles it contains. \Cref{sec:bare-graph-not-enough} shows that this fails in both directions: there are acyclic graphs that are not confluent and cyclic graphs that are. The reason is simple. Confluence compares pairs of rewrite sequences leaving the same state, and its local form, local confluence, compares pairs of one-step rewrites; by Newman's lemma, the local form suffices for terminating systems. Such a pair of one-step rewrites, a \emph{peak}, is not a cycle. It becomes the boundary of a closed region only once the two branches have been brought back together.

This suggests recording the peaks explicitly. Following the higher-dimensional view of rewriting \cite{Mimram2023,GuiraudMalbos2018,GuiraudMalbos2012,Squier1987}, we treat states as vertices, one-step rewrites as edges, and each peak as a candidate two-dimensional cell. When the two branches of a peak have a common descendant, the peak and its joining paths form a closed boundary and the cell can be filled; we then say that the peak has trivial \emph{elementary holonomy}. When no common descendant exists, the boundary stays open and the holonomy is nontrivial (\Cref{fig:intro-filled-open}). The resulting structure, which we call a reduction 2-complex, is the object on which the flatness reading of confluence becomes literal.

With these definitions the local correspondence is exact: a system is \emph{elementarily flat}, meaning that every peak has trivial holonomy, if and only if it is locally confluent (\Cref{thm:elementary-flatness-iff-local-confluence}). The proof is short because the definitions are designed to match. Its value lies less in its difficulty than in fixing a precise dictionary. That dictionary becomes more informative when combined with termination. By Newman's lemma, a terminating system that is elementarily flat is confluent, and, for a terminating system, confluence, elementary flatness, and uniqueness of reachable normal forms are equivalent (\Cref{sec:core-theorem}). We prove these statements for arbitrary abstract rewriting systems and check them in Lean~4 (\Cref{app:lean-formal-core}).

Concrete rewriting formalisms add one further layer. In string and term rewriting, most peaks are harmless: two rewrites at disjoint positions commute, and a rewrite inside the substituted part of another redex can be absorbed. The only peaks that can fail to be joinable come from genuine overlaps of left-hand sides, which are recorded by \emph{critical pairs} \cite{KnuthBendix1970,Huet1980,BaaderNipkow1998}. Critical pairs therefore generate the nontrivial part of the holonomy question (\Cref{sec:critical-pairs-curvature}). In computations we work relative to chosen start terms and use the \emph{reachable} critical-pair instances: concrete overlaps, possibly inside a larger context and under a substitution, that occur in terms reachable from the starts.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We show by explicit small counterexamples that the two natural cycle-based notions of flatness for reduction graphs are unrelated to confluence (\Cref{prop:bare-graph-not-diagnostic}), and we explain why: peaks are not cycles.
    \item We define reduction 2-complexes and elementary holonomy as a fillability status of peaks, and prove that elementary flatness is equivalent to local confluence (\Cref{thm:elementary-flatness-iff-local-confluence}). Under termination we obtain the global form: confluence, elementary flatness, and unique normal forms coincide (\Cref{cor:terminating-confluence-iff-flat}, \Cref{thm:normalizing-confluence-iff-unf}). All results are mechanized in Lean~4 for arbitrary rewriting relations.
    \item We place critical pairs in this picture as the generators of the nontrivial holonomy obligations in string and term rewriting, and distinguish them from peaks that are joinable for structural reasons (\Cref{sec:critical-pairs-curvature}).
    \item We test the picture computationally: an exhaustive enumeration of all rewriting relations on one to four labeled states, smallest counterexamples for each dropped hypothesis, and curated string and left-linear term-rewriting examples, including an overlap below the root (\Cref{sec:computational-bridge}).
    \item We read two explicit completion steps, one for strings and one for terms, as holonomy elimination: the branching survives, its filler appears, and confluence is restored (\Cref{sec:completion-holonomy}).
\end{enumerate}

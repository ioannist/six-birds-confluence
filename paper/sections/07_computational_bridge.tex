\section{Computational Evidence}
\label{sec:computational-bridge}

The computations serve two purposes. For finite abstract rewriting systems, where the results of \Cref{sec:core-theorem} are theorems, they check the implementation against the theorems and locate the smallest systems showing that each hypothesis is needed. For string and term rewriting, they test the start-relative critical-pair picture of \Cref{sec:critical-pairs-curvature} on curated examples. The implementation, the generated records, and the regression tests are described in \Cref{app:computational-protocol,app:artifact-guide}.

\subsection{Exhaustive enumeration of small systems}
\label{subsec:exhaustive-finite-ars-audit}

We enumerate every rewriting relation on $n$ labeled states for $1 \leq n \leq 4$, self-loops included. There are $2^{n^2}$ such relations, giving
\[
2^{1} + 2^{4} + 2^{9} + 2^{16} = 66{,}066
\]
systems in all. A finite system is terminating exactly when its graph has no directed cycle, so the terminating systems are the labeled directed acyclic graphs; there are $1$, $3$, $25$, and $543$ of them for $n = 1,\dots,4$, in agreement with the known count of labeled acyclic digraphs, for $572$ in total. Of these, $1$, $3$, $22$, and $401$ are confluent.

For each terminating system we compute elementary flatness from the nonjoinable peaks, and independently compute local confluence, confluence, and uniqueness of reachable normal forms. \Cref{thm:elementary-flatness-iff-local-confluence}, \Cref{cor:terminating-confluence-iff-flat}, and \Cref{thm:normalizing-confluence-iff-unf} predict that these four properties agree on every terminating system. They do: all four discrepancy counts in \Cref{tab:exhaustive-finite-ars-summary} are zero. Since the statements are proved, the value of this computation is as a test of the implementation, which the string and term computations reuse. As a further check, an independent implementation based on Boolean-matrix transitive closure recomputes termination, local confluence, confluence, and unique normal forms for all $66{,}066$ systems, cyclic ones included, and agrees with the main implementation on every one.

\subsection{Smallest counterexamples}
\label{subsec:boundary-search}

The same enumeration, now over all $66{,}066$ systems rather than only the terminating ones, searches for systems that refute plausible strengthenings of the results. For each statement it reports a smallest witness, first by number of states and then by number of edges. All six statements in \Cref{tab:boundary-counterexamples} are refuted. Together they show that the hypotheses of \Cref{sec:core-theorem} cannot be dropped and that the shortcuts of \Cref{sec:bare-graph-not-enough} fail.

The first witness is worth drawing (\Cref{fig:newman-needs-termination}). It is locally confluent, and in fact every state reaches a normal form, but the cycle between $s_0$ and $s_1$ lets the system drift toward either of two distinct normal forms. It is the classical example showing that Newman's lemma needs termination \cite{Huet1980,BaaderNipkow1998}.

\subsection{Curated string systems}
\label{subsec:curated-string-systems}

The four string examples were chosen to exercise the start-relative critical-pair picture: a confluent sorting system ($E005$), a nonconfluent overlap with an irrelevant second critical pair ($E006$), and a before/after completion pair ($E007$, $E008$). Each was explored from its start word until no new words appeared, so the explored graph is the complete set $X_S$ of reachable words. \Cref{tab:curated-string-summary} reports the results. In every case the number of nonjoinable reachable critical-pair instances is zero exactly when the system is confluent from its start word.

\subsection{Curated left-linear term systems}
\label{subsec:curated-trs-bridge}

The four term examples are a small Peano-addition system with no critical pairs ($TRS001$), a root overlap ($TRS002$), and the nested-overlap pair $TRS003$--$TRS004$, whose overlap lies below the root. \subsection{When a computation certifies a verdict}
\label{subsec:certification}

Exploring a string or term rewriting system from a start term may not terminate, so the explorer works under explicit bounds on depth, number of states, and term size. Absence of a common descendant within the bounds does not prove that none exists. A truncated exploration of the diamond $a \to b$, $a \to c$, $b \to d$, $c \to d$ that stops after one step, for instance, sees $b$ and $c$ but not $d$. The implementation therefore separates two kinds of output. Diagnostics computed on an explored graph, such as its peaks and their joinability within the graph, are always available but are labeled as properties of the explored graph and are marked as certified for the reachable system only when the exploration was complete. The critical-pair, holonomy, and completion analyses on which the tables of this paper rest refuse to run on an incomplete exploration and report the bounds as inconclusive instead; where they also compute critical pairs from the rules, they require that computation to be complete as well. Completeness here refers to the reachable set $X_S$, not to all words or terms. All curated examples above meet this requirement.

The inputs are also validated before analysis. For term rewriting, a rule may not have a variable as its left-hand side or introduce variables on its right-hand side that do not occur on its left, function symbols must be used with consistent arities, and the critical-pair generator accepts only left-linear rules. One-step rewriting itself supports non-left-linear matching.
